**Receive & Accept** is the first stage of your board. Florist orders
land here once they're sent. Your job: have a look and accept (or, in
rare cases, reject).

<HelpMedia id="receive-and-accept-view" kind="video" caption="Reviewing an incoming order in Receive & Accept" />

## What's on the page

Receive & Accept shows incoming orders that are **Sent** or
already **Accepted** but haven't moved on yet. For each, you'll see:

- The florist's name and organisation
- Delivery date
- Total value
- Number of stems
- A status badge

Click into any order to see the full breakdown, flower by flower,
before you accept.

## Accepting an order

<Steps>

1. Click into an order to see the full detail — every event, every arrangement, every flower.

2. Have a look through:

   - Can you source everything by the delivery date?
   - Anything unusual you want to confirm — specific varieties, spares, notes?
   - Do the quantities look right?

3. Click **Accept Order**.

   <HelpMedia id="accept-order-button" />

4. You'll see a confirmation: **"Order W-1234 has been accepted and moved to Assign & Send."** (with your real order reference in place of W-1234)

5. The order disappears from this column and lands in **Assign & Send**.

</Steps>

<Aside type="note">
When you accept, you accept the **whole order**. There's no way to
accept some flowers and decline others. If one flower's tricky, accept
the order, then either talk it through with your florist or substitute
that flower later (once the substitution feature ships).
</Aside>

## Rejecting an order

Rejecting sends the whole order back to the florist as a draft. They
can edit and re-send, or call you to talk it through.

<Steps>

1. Open the order detail view.

2. Click **Reject Order**.

3. Confirm. You'll see: **"Order W-1234 has been rejected."** (with your real order reference in place of W-1234)

4. The order disappears from your board.

</Steps>

Try to accept rather than reject where you can — a quick call with
your florist usually sorts most issues without taking the order off
your board.

## When the page is empty

If there's nothing to process, you'll see:

> No orders found
>
> No assigned orders are currently available

That's normal at the start of a week or right after a busy session.

## What "accepted" looks like for the florist

The moment you accept, the florist's view of the order flips to
**Accepted** and they get an email letting them know. From here they're
waiting for **Supply Confirmed** to appear next — which happens once
you've worked the order through *Assign & Send*.

## Cut-offs

Each wholesaler has a **cut-off time** for accepting orders. Anything
that arrives after the cut-off may need to wait for the next sourcing
window. StemCounts doesn't enforce a cut-off automatically — it's
something you agree with your florists directly.
