A typical florist order spans several flower types, and any one of
them can come from a different supplier. StemCounts handles that for
you by tracking each flower on its own.

<HelpMedia id="multi-supplier-diagram" caption="One florist order → multiple supplier orders → reconstituted at handover" />

## How it works in practice

Imagine a florist has placed an order containing:

- 100 stems of red roses
- 50 stems of white lilies
- 200 stems of eucalyptus

You source from three suppliers:

- Roses → **Supplier A**
- Lilies → **Supplier B**
- Eucalyptus → **Supplier C**

Behind the scenes:

1. The florist order = **one** AssignedOrder in StemCounts
2. Each flower aggregates across all your accepted florist orders (so
   if two other florists also ordered red roses, those 100 stems
   combine into a single supplier order line)
3. Three supplier orders are created — one per supplier
4. Each supplier order has its own status (sent → confirmed →
   fulfilled)

## What this means for status tracking

Because every flower is tracked separately, the florist's overall order
status is **whatever the slowest flower says it is**.

So an order with 9 flowers confirmed and 1 still pending shows as
**Sent** on the florist side until that last one catches up.

<Aside type="note">
This is also why the Order Board can show an order in *Confirmed* while
a specific flower is still moving through *Assign & Send* — the
flower-by-flower view is what to trust.
</Aside>

## Partial delivery to the florist

You can mark **some** flowers as ready for the florist while others are
still with suppliers. The florist sees a **Ready** badge against each
individual flower and can come pick up early or wait for the whole order.

This works well for:

- Foliage and base stems available early (sent up front)
- Specialty flowers with longer lead time (sent later)

## When a supplier can't deliver

If a supplier can't deliver something, you've got three options:

1. **Re-assign** to a different supplier — drag the flower back to *Assign & Send* and pick someone else
2. **Substitute** directly with your florist — call or email them, agree the substitution, then update the flower in StemCounts to match what's actually coming
3. **Reject the flower** — sends your florist a notification that the original isn't available

A built-in substitution flow is on the roadmap. For now, options 1 and 2 cover most situations.

## Reporting

The Order Summary view (calendar mode) shows aggregate stem counts
across all suppliers for any selected week. Useful for forecasting
volume.

## Best practices

- For volume stems (roses, eucalyptus), commit to one or two primary suppliers per week
- For unusual stems, lean on your direct supplier relationships; assign in StemCounts once you've confirmed by phone
- Use the *Florist view* in **Confirmed & Ready** to spot when one florist's order is mostly ready but a single flower is lagging
