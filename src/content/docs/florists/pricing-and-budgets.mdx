StemCounts tracks two prices for every flower: the **wholesale cost** you
pay your wholesaler, and the **customer price** your client pays you. A
few rules connect them.

## The two prices

| Price | Who it is for | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Wholesale cost** | What you pay your wholesaler | The wholesaler's own price list. You cannot change it. |
| **Customer price** | What your client pays you | Wholesale cost multiplied by a markup |

Which markup applies is decided in this order:

1. A **Markup override** on the flower line, if you set one.
2. **Price Markup for this Event**, if the event has one.
3. Your organisation's **Retail markup**.

## Your retail markup

Go to **Settings > Your Organisation** and click **Retail markup**. It is
a multiplier, not a percentage: "The multiplier applied to wholesale costs
when pricing for your customers. Example: 3 x $10 = $30." Values snap to
the nearest 0.25, and the row shows it as, for example, "3 x cost".

This is your default for every event. Markup only changes your
customer-side figures. The wholesale cost always comes from the
wholesaler.

<Aside type="caution">
Customer figures on screen use your current markup (or the event's) at
the time you look at them. Changing the markup changes the customer
figures shown on existing events too. The wholesale cost of a sent order
is not affected.
</Aside>

## A different markup for one event

Open **Edit Event Details** from the pencil beside the event name (or
**Edit** on the event card). Enter a multiplier in **Price Markup for this
Event**, for example 3.5, then click **Save Changes**. Values snap to the
nearest 0.25. Leave the field empty to go back to your default markup.

## A different markup for one flower

While the order is a draft, click the flower's name on an arrangement
card. A card shows **Wholesale / bunch** and **Customer / bunch**. Enter a
multiplier in **Markup override** and click **Save**. Use **Clear
override** to go back to the event or default markup. A flower with its
own markup shows its name in coral, and hovering shows "Markup: Nx (this
stem)". Useful for premium stems where your usual markup does not fit.

## Budgets

An arrangement's budget is your customer price for one of that design,
ex GST. The arrangement screens say "All budget values are Ex GST", so
enter the ex GST figure you would quote your client. The event header
adds up budgets multiplied by quantities, and its **Spent** includes
Spares.

Switch between **Wholesale** (your cost) and **Customer** (cost plus
markup). **Flower Profit** is the customer budget minus your cost. The
info button (**How these numbers work**) explains that **Remaining**
compares your customer price with the budget, while profit compares the
budget with your cost, so you can be over budget and still profitable.
Going over budget never blocks you.

## GST

Budgets, spend and profit on the event and order pages are worked out
from your ex GST budgets and your wholesalers' listed prices, so treat
them as ex GST figures. Quoting a client including GST? Add 10% to the
budget yourself.

## What the wholesaler sees

The order PDF the wholesaler receives is a picking document: flowers,
colours, stems and bunches, and any comments you left. It shows no
prices. Your customer prices, markups and budgets stay on your side.

## Revenue This Week

**Revenue This Week** on the home screen is, for orders in the current
week, the wholesale cost of the flowers, rounded up to whole bunches,
multiplied by your organisation's retail markup.

## After a substitute or fewer stems

You are billed for what the wholesaler supplies: the substitute you
accepted at the price shown, or the reduced number of stems. A flower the
wholesaler cannot supply comes off your invoice. See
[Substitutes and supply changes](/florists/substitutes-and-supply-changes/).

## Talking price with your wholesaler

Wholesale prices are set by your wholesaler. If you are ordering in volume
and want a different price, talk to them directly. StemCounts does not
get involved in price conversations.
